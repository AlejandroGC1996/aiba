# Licensing decision

AIBA is released under the MIT License. The decision follows the permissive
licensing style used by several other repositories listed by the Cátedra and is
intended to support reuse in research and applied projects.

## Practical options

### MIT License

Short and permissive. It allows reuse, modification, redistribution and
commercial use while requiring preservation of the copyright and license notice.
This is the simplest default for an academic algorithm intended for broad reuse.

### BSD 3-Clause License

Also permissive, with an additional clause preventing use of contributor or
institution names to endorse derived products. This can be attractive when a
university or research group name appears prominently.

### Apache License 2.0

Permissive and more detailed. It includes an explicit patent grant and a NOTICE
mechanism, but is longer and requires slightly more release administration.

### GPL v3

Copyleft: redistributed derivative software must remain under GPL-compatible
terms. Choose it only if preserving openness of downstream derivatives is an
explicit project objective.

## Selected notice

The copyright notice lists José María Vecino Otero, Alejandro González Casal and
Abraham Prieto García. Multiple people may jointly hold copyright and multiple
authors may be credited in the software citation.

Authorship and copyright ownership are distinct. If an employment agreement or
Universidade da Coruña policy assigns ownership to an institution, the copyright
notice should be updated without removing the individual scholarly attribution
recorded in `AUTHORS.md` and `CITATION.cff`.

## Third-party material

The Yacht Hydrodynamics data in the example is independently licensed under
CC BY 4.0. Its license does not determine the license of AIBA itself. Attribution
must remain in the example even after a software license is selected.
